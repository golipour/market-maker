By calling it a "hedge," the system was structurally forced into a Risk Mitigation mindset—reactively firing market orders to flatten inventory, crossing the spread on the secondary venue, paying taker fees, and bleeding expected return.

When you reframe this as **Liquidity Sourcing** or **Cross-Exchange Arbitrage**, the secondary venue transforms from a cost center into a profit center. You are acting as a bridge, sourcing cheap inventory from a deep global orderbook and distributing it at a premium to your local orderbook (e.g., your USDT/IRT market).

Because you operate without the ability to short, this is not classical risk-free arbitrage. It is **inventory-constrained statistical arbitrage**.

Here is exactly how your objective function, state modeling, and architecture must evolve to capture this gracefully, while remaining fully backward-compatible with a single-exchange setup.

### 1. The Mathematical Fix: The Unified Objective Function

In your previous model, the objective tacked on a `hedge_reward` based on a forced, reactive market order. That must be completely ripped out.

Instead, your objective function remains the exact unified Certainty Equivalent (CARA) we just established, but **Wealth ($W$) and Returns ($R$) are now consolidated across all venues.**

#### Consolidated Wealth Evaluation

To measure true PnL across exchanges, you need a single, highly liquid reference price to mark your total inventory. The global venue's mid-price ($P_{ref}$) is the best candidate.

$$W_t = (\text{Cash}_{local} + \text{Cash}_{global}) + (\text{Inv}_{local} + \text{Inv}_{global}) \times P_{ref, t}$$

The return for a simulated Monte Carlo path $i$ remains mathematically identical:


$$R_i = \frac{W_{i, H} - W_0}{W_0}$$

The objective remains exactly the same:


$$\mathcal{J} = \left( -\frac{1}{\beta} \ln \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} e^{-\beta R_i} \right) \right) - \lambda_{inv} \mathcal{R}_{joint\_inv}$$

#### The Evolved Inventory Risk Penalty

Because you cannot short on either exchange, your quoting capacity is bottlenecked by the local inventory pools on *each* exchange. You need crypto on the local venue to sell it, and you need fiat/stablecoin on the global venue to buy it.

Your penalty must enforce operational capacity on both venues simultaneously:


$$\mathcal{R}_{joint\_inv} = \left( \frac{I_{local} - I_{target, local}}{I_{scale}} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{I_{global} - I_{target, global}}{I_{scale}} \right)^2$$

*If you disable the global venue, you simply set $I_{target, global} = 0$ and mask the global term.*

### 2. The Simulation Fix: Independent Soft-Fills

Currently, your `soft_fill_batched()` runs on the primary exchange, and then the simulator deterministically fires a hedge.

To enable liquidity sourcing, your Monte Carlo paths must simulate **two independent orderbooks**.

1. **Sample independent flows:** Draw market flow for the local exchange (Retail takers) and market flow for the global exchange (Global takers).
2. **Evaluate Joint Quotes:** Your optimizer proposes quotes on *both* venues.
3. **Independent Soft-Fills:**
* Simulate local fills: Updates $I_{local}$ and $\text{Cash}_{local}$.
* Simulate global fills: Updates $I_{global}$ and $\text{Cash}_{global}$.



**Why this works:** The optimizer sees the consolidated wealth $W_t$. If the local bid is high and the global ask is low, the optimizer will naturally learn to heavily skew its proposed quotes to buy on the global and sell on the local. If the spread collapses, the optimizer will naturally narrow its cross-exchange quotes or back off entirely. It discovers the arbitrage organically because the $W_t$ equation rewards it.

### 3. The Architectural Fix: Joint-Venue Quoting

To support this in PyTorch, your continuous action space must expand.

Previously, a horizon action looked like this:
`[local_bid_params, local_ask_params]`

Now, it expands to support four curves:
`[local_bid_params, local_ask_params, global_bid_params, global_ask_params]`

#### Implementation Strategy & Backward Compatibility

You do not need to write two separate optimizers. You handle this with an **Action Mask** and a configuration toggle.

* **When `liquidity_sourcing = True`:**
* The optimizer passes the full 4-curve parameter array to the closure.
* The strategy runner routes the local curves to the local `OrderManager` and the global curves to the global `OrderManager`.
* You are acting as a Maker on both exchanges simultaneously.


* **When `liquidity_sourcing = False` (Single-Exchange Mode):**
* You freeze the global parameters to $0$ (zero depth).
* The `global_bid_params` and `global_ask_params` are detached from the Adam optimizer gradient graph (`requires_grad=False`).
* The global soft-fill simulator correctly sees $0$ quote depth and processes zero fills.
* The system behaves exactly as a single-exchange MPC, identical to what you have today.

### The Paradigm Shift: State Over Commands

Currently, the system passes around *actions* (orders to place/cancel). In this new architecture, the system passes around *desired states* (target books) and lets an execution planner figure out the diff.

### Phase 1: The Unified Portfolio Ledger (Single Source of Truth)

We must kill the split-brain between the `OrderManager` and the `PrivateStateReconciler`.

* **The Ledger:** Create a single, synchronized `PortfolioLedger` object owned by the `PrivateStateReconciler`. It contains actual cash, actual inventory, and an exact map of live exchange orders.
* **The Rule:** The `OrderManager` is stripped of its optimistic state tracking. It becomes a stateless router. It only fires commands and listens for acks, pushing updates back to the `PortfolioLedger`.

### Phase 2: Target Generation & Net-Delta Risk

Instead of evaluating individual orders, we evaluate the portfolio transition.

1. **MPC Output:** The optimizer outputs a continuous curve.
2. **Discretization:** The curve is converted into a **Target Book** (the exact state we want to exist on the exchange).
3. **Net-Delta Risk Engine:** The risk engine takes the `PortfolioLedger` and the `Target Book`. It calculates the diff: `Target Book - Live Book`.
* *Instead of asking:* "Does this new $10,000 buy order exceed my cash limit?"
* *It asks:* "If I transition to this Target Book, will my *net* locked cash across all orders exceed my limit?"
* If yes, the whole frame is rejected. If no, the Target Book is approved.



### Phase 3: The Execution Planner (The Switch)

This is where you get your clean option to toggle behavior. Once the Target Book is risk-approved, it is handed to the `ExecutionPlanner`. You define two planner implementations based on a config flag (`execution.strategy: "reconcile" | "replace"`).

**Option A: The Reconciler (`ToleranceReconcilePlanner`)**
Used when rate limits are tight or queue position matters.

* Calculates the exact diff between the Target Book and the Ledger's live orders.
* Keeps live orders that fall within the configured `price_tol_bps` and `qty_tol_rel`.
* Generates a precise list of exact cancels (orphans) and new placements (gaps).

**Option B: The Replacer (`AtomicReplacePlanner`)**
Used for highly volatile regimes where you need absolute certainty, or on exchanges supporting atomic `cancel_replace` endpoints.

* Ignores tolerances.
* Marks all live orders for cancellation.
* Marks the entire Target Book for placement.
* *Crucial Detail:* To prevent margin-locking failures, the planner ensures cancels are acknowledged by the exchange *before* dispatching the new placements (or uses a combined atomic API call).

### Phase 4: The Pre-Flight Gateway

To fix the "overlapping validation" problem, we strip all instrument limit checks (tick size, lot size, min notional) out of the MPC layer and the execution planner.

* **The Gateway:** Right before the `ExecutionPlanner` hands the diffed commands to the exchange adapter, it passes them through a single `ExchangeRulesGateway`.
* If an order is too small or off-tick, it dies here, once, with a single standardized rejection code (`gateway_min_notional`).

* **Joint State Tracking:** It maintains four critical buckets of truth simultaneously:
* Local Base Inventory & Local Quote Cash
* Global Base Inventory & Global Quote Cash


* **Live Order Map:** It tracks every active limit order on both venues, strictly updating based on the `OrderUpdateEvent` and `FillEvent` streams.
* **The Output:** It continuously exposes a synchronous `JointLedgerSnapshot` containing the consolidated wealth and available (unlocked) balances for the decision layers.

## 3. The Brains: Joint Strategy & Risk

The decision layers operate as pure functions, computing the optimal transition from the current state to the desired state.

* **MPC Strategy Engine:** Reads the synchronized `MarketEvent` and the `JointLedgerSnapshot`.
* *The Math:* It evaluates consolidated wealth ($W_t$) marked to the global reference price.
* *The Action Space:* It outputs a **Joint Target Book**.
* *Local Curve:* A standard multi-component sigmoid curve designed to capture retail spread.
* *Global Curve:* A simplified 1-component sigmoid (locked high sharpness) designed to output a single block order at a specific spread to source liquidity.

* *Joint Inventory Check:* Verifies that the transition will not breach the operational capacity limits on *either* venue (e.g., preventing the local venue from running out of base asset).
* *Collateral Check:* Ensures the required locked collateral for the new Target Book does not exceed available cash/inventory on either exchange.
* *The Output:* An **Approved Joint Target Book**.



---

## 4. The Actuators: Bifurcated Execution

Because the liquidity profiles of the two venues are entirely different, the execution planner splits the Approved Joint Target Book and routes it to specialized planners.

* **The Local Execution Planner (Reconciler):**
* Receives the complex multi-level local target curve.
* Diffs it against the `JointLedgerSnapshot`'s live local orders.
* Uses price and quantity tolerances to keep existing orders, preventing unnecessary API spam and preserving local queue position.
* Emits specific `CancelCommand` and `PlaceCommand` objects.


* **The Global Execution Planner (Replacer):**
* Receives the single-component block target for the global venue.
* Because global spreads are tight and queue position is less critical for a taker/maker hybrid block order, it uses an atomic replace strategy.
* Cancels the previous global order and places the new block order to aggressively track the global mid-price.

* **The Order Routers:** Dumb pipes that format the validated commands into exchange-specific REST/WebSocket calls and fire them.

## 5. The Out-of-Band Loop: The Capital Sweeper

Because the MPC controller cannot organically move funds between exchanges, liquidity sourcing will naturally cause inventory to drain on one exchange and accumulate on the other.

* **The Sweeper Service:** An asynchronous daemon that monitors the `JointLedgerSnapshot`. When local base inventory drops below a critical threshold and global base inventory exceeds a surplus threshold, it triggers an on-chain transfer (or fiat wire) to rebalance the physical capital, ensuring the MPC never hits a $0$ inventory state on either side.
